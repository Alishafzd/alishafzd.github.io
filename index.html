Ali Shafiezadeh

I am a graduate researcher in the University of Alberta, where I work with Professor Ehsan Hashemi on robotics and autonomous navigation. My research centers on perception-driven state estimation, mapping, and multi-robot autonomy for field and indoor platforms.

I build end-to-end autonomy stacks that span embedded sensing, middleware integration, and simulation. Recent efforts include commissioning outdoor autonomous vehicle platforms, deploying mobile indoor robots for resilient navigation, and designing high-performance RC platforms to explore racing autonomy workflows. I was also part of the lead executive team for the Intelligent Transportation Systems Conference 2024, highlighted in this video.

Previously, I completed a year-long co-op with Ledcor Inc. on the safety of autonomous trucks, focusing on sensor fusion and state estimation, and I now serve as a lead researcher at NODE Lab, where we are building scalable, real-world autonomy stacks that tightly integrate perception, mapping, and decision-making. I enjoy translating experimental insights into deployable systems that accelerate autonomy research.

Featured Projects

NODE Lab Autonomous Platform Commissioning

Led the setup of NODE Lab's full-scale autonomous vehicle platform, integrating sensor calibration, multi-sensor perception, and modular middleware for safe outdoor navigation. Delivered a repeatable deployment playbook for research teams and defined validation workflows that span simulation and proving-ground trials.

Highlights: Multi-modal sensor synchronization, ROS 2 upgrade path, real-time monitoring dashboards.
Indoor Mobile Robot Fleet Enablement

Configured mobile robots such as Clearpath Husky, Ridgeback, MK-mini Yuhesen, etc. robots for collaborative indoor autonomy. Developed standardized sensor payloads, built mapping and localization pipelines, developed object detection and tracking modules, and authored operator guides that allow rapid switching between research experiments.

Highlights: Continuous integration for ROS workspaces, fleet health monitoring, extensible teleoperation toolkit.
F1 RC Car Development & Isaac Sim Pipeline

Designed a high-speed RC-scale F1 platform covering electronics, embedded software, and photorealistic simulation. Built a synchronized workflow between Isaac Sim and onboard compute for rapid perception and planning experiments.

Highlights: Custom PCB and firmware stack, teleoperation setup, SolidWorks into Isaac Sim, and finally Isaac Lab training.
